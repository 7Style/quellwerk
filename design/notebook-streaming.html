Article 9 Risk management system

A risk management system shall be established, implemented, documented and maintained in relation to high-risk AI systems. The risk management system shall be understood as a continuous iterative process planned and run throughout the entire lifecycle of the high-risk AI system, requiring regular systematic review and updating.

It shall comprise the identification and analysis of the known and the reasonably foreseeable risks that the high-risk AI system can pose to health, safety or fundamental rights when used in accordance with its intended purpose.

Article 10 Data and data governance

High-risk AI systems which make use of techniques involving the training of AI models shall be developed on the basis of data sets that meet the quality criteria set out below. Training, validation and testing data sets shall be relevant, sufficiently representative, and to the best extent possible free of errors and complete in view of the intended purpose.

Data sets shall take into account, to the extent required by the intended purpose, the characteristics or elements that are particular to the specific geographical, contextual, behavioural or functional setting within which the high-risk AI system is intended to be used.

What must providers do before placing a high-risk system on the market?

Before a high-risk AI system is placed on the market or put into service, providers must establish, implement, document and maintain a risk management system and keep it current for as long as the system remains available.

In addition, the technical documentation shall be drawn up before the system is placed on the market and kept at the disposal of national competent authorities for ten years afterwards.

Who counts as a provider?

A provider is the natural or legal person that develops an AI system, or that has one developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name or trademark, whether for payment or free of charge.

Readiness gaps, internal memo

The triage model has been in production since January. This memo lists what we would not be able to show an auditor today.

Documentation is partly in place: we have a model card and an evaluation report, but we have no documented post-market monitoring plan for the triage model and no owner assigned for incident reporting.

Data governance is the second gap. The training set was assembled in 2022 from three hospital systems and we cannot currently demonstrate how representative it is for the population we serve.

What does the regulation require for post-market monitoring?

Providers have to plan for what happens after the system is in use, not only before it ships. The risk management system runs throughout the entire lifecycle of the high-risk AI system, which is the same clause that carries the monitoring duty.

Your memo records the gap on this point: there is no documented post-market monitoring plan for the triage model.
