Article 9 Risk management system

A risk management system shall be established, implemented, documented and maintained in relation to high-risk AI systems. The risk management system shall be understood as a continuous iterative process planned and run throughout the entire lifecycle of the high-risk AI system, requiring regular systematic review and updating.

It shall comprise the identification and analysis of the known and the reasonably foreseeable risks that the high-risk AI system can pose to health, safety or fundamental rights when used in accordance with its intended purpose.

Article 10 Data and data governance

High-risk AI systems which make use of techniques involving the training of AI models shall be developed on the basis of data sets that meet the quality criteria set out below. Training, validation and testing data sets shall be relevant, sufficiently representative, and to the best extent possible free of errors and complete in view of the intended purpose.

Data sets shall take into account, to the extent required by the intended purpose, the characteristics or elements that are particular to the specific geographical, contextual, behavioural or functional setting within which the high-risk AI system is intended to be used.

What must providers do before placing a high-risk system on the market?

Before a high-risk AI system is placed on the market or put into service, providers must establish, implement, document and maintain a risk management system and keep it current for as long as the system remains available.

In addition, the technical documentation shall be drawn up before the system is placed on the market and kept at the disposal of national competent authorities for ten years afterwards.

Who counts as a provider?

A provider is the natural or legal person that develops an AI system, or that has one developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name or trademark, whether for payment or free of charge.

Provider obligations for high-risk AI systems, and where we stand

Executive summary

The material covers one regulatory regime and one internal readiness check against it. Three points carry the rest. First, the duties attach before a system reaches the market: a risk management system must be in place at that point, and so must the technical documentation. Second, the duty does not end at launch, because the risk management system runs throughout the entire lifecycle. Third, our own memo records a gap against exactly that continuing duty.

Main themes

Obligations that must be met before market placement

The Commission material states the sequence plainly: the risk management system is established, implemented, documented and maintained before the system is placed on the market or put into service. The technical documentation follows the same rule and is drawn up beforehand, not retrospectively.

Data governance as a separate obligation

For systems built on training, the requirement reaches into the data itself: training, validation and testing sets must be relevant and sufficiently representative for the intended purpose.

Our readiness against the list

The internal memo is candid about the shortfall: there is no documented post-market monitoring plan for the triage model.

Key facts and figures

  • Technical documentation must exist before market placement.
  • The risk management system is a continuous iterative process, not a one-time exercise.
  • Data sets must be relevant and sufficiently representative for the intended purpose.

Notable quotes

Providers must establish, implement, document and maintain a risk management system.
We have no documented post-market monitoring plan for the triage model.

Open questions and gaps

The sources set the duties and record one gap, but they do not connect the two in time.

Not covered by the sources: the deadline by which the monitoring plan has to exist, who owns incident reporting after the plan is written, and what the penalty would be if the gap remains open.

Conclusion

Two of the four pre-market duties are documented on our side; the continuing duty is the one with no owner and no plan. Closing that gap is the shortest path from the material to a defensible position.
